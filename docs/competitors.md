# Competitors — what tattoo sites do (Nov 2026)

## What the good ones share
- Dark, moody branding with very large type; the site works as a gallery, not a brochure.
- Portfolio first: big images, filter by style, each artist with bio + social links.
- Booking is a **request with a brief**, not a calendar click: placement, size, style, budget, reference images.
- Two steps: consultation first, then the session. Deposit taken to cut no-shows.
- Aftercare info and consent handled online.

## Where they are weak (our openings)
- Most are templates (Squarespace / Framer / WordPress): same layout, little motion, no story.
- "About" is a paragraph. Nobody shows the *process* — which is what nervous first-timers want to see.
- Idea tools on the web are generic AI generators or personality quizzes. They are not tied to a real
  artist's portfolio, and most ignore skin tone. Sources themselves say a professional must adjust for
  anatomy, line weight, skin tone and ageing.
- Pricing is hidden or rigid. Nobody says "this is a starting point, let's talk".

## What InkLusion does differently
1. Scroll story: the artist, then a tattoo literally being drawn as you scroll through the process.
2. Idea finder that gives a direction + the reason, then shows the artist's own matching pieces.
3. Booking request that carries the idea-finder result, so the artist gets the brief for free.
4. Honest pricing: starting points, stated as negotiable.

## Later, worth adding
Deposit payment (needs a payment provider), aftercare page, reviews, healed-vs-fresh photos.
